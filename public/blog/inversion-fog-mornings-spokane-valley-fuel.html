Inversion Season: Fueling Through Spokane Valley's Gray, Foggy Mornings

By the Everbowl Spokane kitchen · November 10, 2026 · 5 min read

Drive up Sullivan Road or climb toward Beacon Hill some mornings this time of year, and you'll notice something odd: the fog you just left behind on Sprague Ave is nowhere to be seen. That's not your imagination — it's a temperature inversion, and it's one of the Inland Northwest's most reliable mid-November weather patterns. Cold, dense air sinks into the Spokane Valley overnight and gets capped by a layer of warmer air above it, trapping fog, moisture, and haze down low while the surrounding hills sit in clear, sunny air just a few hundred feet up. The valley floor can stay socked in gray until midday or later, even when the forecast technically says "mostly sunny."

It's a genuinely striking local phenomenon, but it's also the kind of weather that quietly saps people's energy without them noticing why. Low light, damp cold air, and a sky that looks the same shade of gray from 7am to noon all nudge the body toward sluggishness — and if your eating and hydration habits don't adjust for it, that sluggishness compounds.

Why foggy, damp cold hits differently than dry cold

Dense, moisture-laden air at freezing or near-freezing temperatures pulls heat away from your body efficiently, which means an inversion morning can feel colder than the thermometer suggests. Your body responds by working a little harder just to stay warm, even on an ordinary commute or school drop-off. At the same time, cold air blunts the thirst signal the same way it does in a snowstorm, so people tend to under-hydrate through gray stretches without realizing it — they're not sweating, so they assume they're fine. Add low, flat light that can leave you feeling foggier than the weather outside, and skipping a real breakfast in favor of coffee alone is a fast way to hit a mid-morning slump.

The inversion isn't dangerous — it's just the kind of low-grade drain that's easy to blame on "winter tiredness" when the real fix is a lot simpler than waiting for the sun to break through.

Eating for the gray stretch, not against it

The fix isn't complicated: treat foggy mornings the way you'd treat any cold-weather day, with a warm, protein-forward breakfast instead of grabbing something cold and thin on the way out the door. A bowl of Hot Oats — warm, fiber-rich, gluten-free — does more to steady your morning than a pastry and black coffee ever will, and it holds up well if your drive into work or across town is slower than usual because of low visibility. Pair it with a Cacao Ice Blended Coffee, which gives you real caffeine for the fog-driven slow commute along with actual food — cacao, banana, protein — instead of just hot liquid and nothing else.

If you're more of a grab-and-go person on inversion mornings, a smoothie still beats skipping breakfast entirely. Something like a PB Cacao Dream travels well in a cup holder and gives you protein and slow-digesting carbs that won't leave you crashing by 10am, right about the time the fog is finally supposed to burn off.

A simple plan for inversion days

  • Check which kind of gray it is. If the hills around the valley — Mica Peak, the South Hill, Beacon Hill — are sunny while Sprague Ave is still fogged in, that's your inversion sign: it may stay gray at ground level for hours longer than the forecast implies.
  • Eat something warm before you drive. Slower commutes and reduced visibility are stressful enough without doing it on an empty stomach.
  • Hydrate like it's any other cold day. Damp air hides thirst just as effectively as dry cold does; drink water or Iced Matcha even if you don't feel parched.
  • Don't let the gray sky talk you into skipping breakfast. It's tempting to treat a dim morning as an excuse to stay in bed an extra five minutes instead of eating — that trade usually costs more energy than it saves.

The bottom line

Spokane Valley's mid-November inversions are a genuine local quirk — fog trapped low while the hills above sit in sunshine — and they're easy to blame for feeling tired without realizing eating and hydration are the actual lever you can pull.
