Winter Sports Tryouts: Fueling Spokane Valley Student-Athletes Through November's Practice Grind

By the Everbowl Spokane kitchen · November 4, 2026 · 5 min read

November flips a switch in Spokane Valley gyms. Fall sports wrap up, and within a week or two, basketball, wrestling, and swim programs at Central Valley, University, and West Valley High School are running tryouts and the first weeks of preseason practice. It's a different kind of demanding than fall ball ever was — two-hour practices after a full school day, five or six nights a week, often ending after dark with a drive home and homework still waiting. For a lot of families, this is the point in the year where the fueling routine either holds together or quietly falls apart.

Why early winter season is its own problem

Fall sports usually have a game clock parents can plan around. Preseason tryouts and early practices are messier: start times can shift night to night, cuts and roster-building add stress on top of the physical load, and the short November daylight means kids are doing conditioning drills and full-court sprints in a gym that was dark outside before practice even started. Add a school night on the other end, and "what's for dinner" becomes a real logistics question around 5pm, not a relaxed one.

A fueling plan that survives a real Tuesday

  • After school, before practice: a small carb-forward snack an hour or two out — not a full meal right before sprint lines. Toast, fruit, or a smoothie all beat a vending machine candy bar for steady energy through a two-hour session.
  • During long tryouts: water, consistently, especially for wrestlers managing effort in a warm room or swimmers who don't feel how much they're sweating in the pool.
  • Right after practice: this is the window that actually matters most on a school night — carbs to refill what got burned, plus protein to support recovery, ideally within the hour so a tired kid isn't running on fumes for homework.
  • Don't skip the actual dinner. A recovery smoothie or bowl is a great bridge, not a replacement for a real meal once everyone's home.
The best practice-night food is the food that's actually ready when the car pulls in. A perfect meal plan that never gets executed on a Tuesday at 7:45pm doesn't help anybody.

The bottom line

Winter tryout season asks a lot of young athletes in a short window: new coaches, new expectations, and real physical load layered on top of a full school day. Food isn't going to make the cut list or the lineup, but a kid who's actually fueled shows up to the gym with more in the tank — and that's worth building a simple, repeatable routine around before the season gets any busier.
