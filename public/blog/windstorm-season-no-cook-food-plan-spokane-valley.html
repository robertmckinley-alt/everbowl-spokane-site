Windstorm Season Prep: A No-Cook Food Plan for Spokane Valley's Fall Power Outages

By the Everbowl Spokane kitchen · November 8, 2026 · 5 min read

Late fall is when the Inland Northwest earns its reputation for wind. Gusty systems roll through Spokane Valley and the rest of the region from November into winter, and every so often one is strong enough to drop a branch on a line and knock out power to a neighborhood for a few hours — sometimes longer. It's rarely dramatic enough to make you stock a bunker, but it's common enough that having a loose plan for "what do we eat if the stove and fridge are out" is worth more than most people give it credit for.

The problem with most "emergency food"

Open most household emergency kits and you'll find crackers, canned soup that needs a stove to be appetizing, and a flashlight. None of that is wrong, exactly, but it's built around calories, not around actually feeling okay. A power outage already comes with its own low-grade stress — kids asking when the lights will come back, a fridge you're trying not to open too often, a phone you're rationing battery on. Eating something that's just carbs and salt on top of that stress doesn't help the way a real meal does.

Build around what needs zero power

The easiest fix is keeping a short list of shelf-stable, protein-and-fiber items that taste fine cold and don't ask anything of your kitchen: nut butter, whole fruit, trail mix, shelf-stable milk or protein, and chia seeds you can soak the night before a forecasted windstorm so they're ready to eat straight out of the fridge before it even loses power. None of that requires a stove, a working outlet, or a generator — just a little bit of planning before the gusts show up.

It's worth doing this prep even for a storm that never actually knocks your power out. Weather forecasts for wind events are reasonably reliable a day or two out, which gives you a real window to soak chia, pick up shelf-stable staples, and charge the small things that matter before the gusts arrive — instead of improvising in the dark with whatever's already in the pantry.

A no-cook plan isn't about surviving a long emergency. It's about not spending an outage eating crackers and feeling worse than you need to.

A short checklist worth keeping on the fridge

  • Before a windy forecast: Soak a batch of chia pudding or grab a pre-ordered Chia Pudding Bowl so something real is ready cold.
  • Charge small things early: Phone, a backup battery, and a flashlight — done before the wind picks up, not during.
  • Keep the fridge closed: Every open-door check during an outage shortens how long your food stays cold.
