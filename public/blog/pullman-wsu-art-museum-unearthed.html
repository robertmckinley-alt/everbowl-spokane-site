A Slow Look at Unearthed in Pullman

By the Everbowl Spokane kitchen · November 21, 2026 · 3 min read

If the Palouse is on your list for a late-fall day trip, start with one work of art. At Washington State University’s Jordan Schnitzer Museum of Art in Pullman, Jim Hodges’ Unearthed (a work in progress) gives you a reason to take your time. The museum is showing it through June 26, 2027, so you can catch it on a late-November visit or save the idea for another weekend.

Look once from a distance, then look again

Unearthed is a large bronze cast of an uprooted tree stump. From a few steps back, see what the mass of roots does to the outline. Then, from another permitted spot in the gallery, look for one small feature you missed the first time. The places where the roots twist or break can look quite different as you move around the work.

If you’re going with a friend, try spending the first minute looking quietly. Then compare what drew each of you in before reading the wall text together. Did one of you see a tree right away? Did the other start with the texture of the metal? Those are good observations to carry into another gallery. If you only have time for one work, it’s also a satisfying way to finish the visit.

Plan around the Thanksgiving break

Find the museum in the Crimson Cube at 1535 NE Wilson Road on the WSU Pullman campus. The museum’s visitor information lists regular public hours from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. Tuesday through Saturday. It lists Thursday, November 26, 2026, as the start of its Thanksgiving closure and Tuesday, December 1, as the reopening date. Check the same page for directions and parking before leaving. For a short outing, give yourself time to find parking, settle into the gallery, and make a second pass by the sculpture.
