A Free Wizard of Oz Matinee at Spokane’s Garland Theater

By the Everbowl Spokane kitchen · November 26, 2026 · 4 min read

For a family outing after Thanksgiving, go see The Wizard of Oz on the big screen at Spokane’s Garland Theater. The theater lists free 2 p.m. screenings on Friday, November 27, and Saturday, November 28, 2026, at 924 W. Garland Ave. Both are part of the theater’s holiday screenings sponsored by Washington Trust Bank. If it’s someone’s first time at the Garland, leave a few minutes to look at the building before the show.

Pick your afternoon and check the listing

The Garland’s special events page lists both matinees as rated G and says doors open 30 minutes before the movie. That puts doors at 1:30 p.m. Pacific for either date under the posted schedule. The two event pages list a small popcorn special for $3 plus tax. The theater also lists other screenings that weekend, so check the date, time, and free admission note together when you pick your showing.

If you’re going with a few households, agree on which day you’re aiming for and make the entrance your meeting point. Build in time to get settled together. For questions about seating, admission arrangements, or access needs, use the theater’s contact page before you head out.

Look at the Garland before you go inside

The tower makes the theater easy to spot. The Spokane Historic Preservation Office identifies the building as a single-screen theater built in 1945 in the Streamline Moderne style. It points out two exterior details you can look for on arrival. One is the 48-foot tower marquee. The other is the turquoise terra-cotta tilework on the brick front, with flower motifs that are easier to pick out when you stop for a moment.

The theater’s own history recalls that the snack bar was a novelty when the Garland opened in November 1945. Its opening program was a double feature. Eight decades later, the popcorn counter is still one way to make the visit feel different from watching a movie at home.

Give each person a detail to spot on the way in. One can look for the flowers in the tiles while someone else reads the lettering on the sign. The photo here shows what the front looked like in 2016. Look at the shape of the sign and how it sits beside the entrance, then see what details you can recognize from the sidewalk today.

Give a familiar movie a fresh look

The Garland was built just six years after the movie came out. The Library of Congress notes that the 1939 film joined the National Film Registry in its inaugural year, 1989. If a younger viewer only knows the story from a book, this is a chance to hear Judy Garland sing “Over the Rainbow” in a theater.

Even if you know the songs by heart, try watching for the difference between Dorothy’s Kansas scenes and her first moments in Oz. Pay attention to what you can see beyond Dorothy as the color and scenery change. If this is a child’s first viewing, let them discover the change on their own, then ask what they saw first. It could be the road, a costume, or something in the background you’ve never paid much attention to.

On the way out, compare one detail from the theater and one from the film. A photo of the marquee can help you remember the day, but a conversation about the surprise someone noticed on screen is just as good a souvenir. That’s enough to make the matinee feel like an outing of its own on a busy holiday weekend.
