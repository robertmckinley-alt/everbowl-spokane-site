See Sandpoint’s Holiday Tree Light Up

By the Everbowl Spokane kitchen · November 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Sandpoint starts the holiday weekend with a countdown in the middle of downtown. The Greater Sandpoint Chamber of Commerce lists its annual tree lighting for Friday, November 27, 2026, from 5 to 7 p.m. Pacific at Jeff Jones Square, at Oak Street and Third Avenue. Admission is free and open to the public. If you’re driving over from the Spokane area, both cities are on Pacific time, so you can plan around the same clock.

Make the countdown the main event

Give yourself time to park and walk to the square near the start of the event. The Chamber’s 5 to 7 p.m. listing covers the evening as a whole. Check its page again before leaving if you’re trying to arrive for a particular part of the program. The Chamber says Santa will take part in the celebration and lists hot cocoa, cider, and treats. A good plan with young kids is to see the tree come on first, then decide how much of the rest of the evening everyone has energy for.

If you’re meeting another household, pick a corner near the square before you arrive and agree on a time to meet there. It’s easier to regroup at a named corner than to find someone by the tree once everyone is looking in the same direction. Let kids choose a detail to look for when the lights come on. They might notice the top of the tree first or see the lights reflected in a window on the walk back.

Plan the walk from the car

If you’re making the drive from Washington, check the WSDOT travel map and Idaho 511 before you leave. Look at the return route while you’re planning, too. Even if you stay only for the countdown, the drive home will be after dark.

The City of Sandpoint’s parking plan identifies a city lot at Third and Church streets. Use it as one option when you look at the map, and follow the signs for current rates, time limits, and any event restrictions when you arrive. If the closest spaces are full, give yourself room to park a few blocks away so you can walk into the square without rushing the countdown.

On the way in, take note of the streets you crossed. By the time you head back, you’ll be walking with other people leaving the event, and remembering your route makes it easier to keep your group together. If someone in your group needs a shorter walk, agree in advance on where you could safely drop them off near the square and where you’ll meet afterward.

Leave room for an early finish

Standing outside feels different from walking a few downtown blocks. Bring a layer you can add at the square and gloves you’re comfortable wearing while holding a cup or taking a photo. If it’s your first time at the lighting, try watching from the edge of the gathering. You can see the tree from farther back and have more room to step away if someone needs a break.

If the group is ready to head out once the lights are on, pause for one last look as you leave the square. That’s often when a family can get a photo together without spending the countdown looking at a phone. For a longer evening, take another look at the tree after the crowd around you has shifted, then follow your planned route back to the car.
